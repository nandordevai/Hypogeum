From time to time I encounter art that I’d really like to review or recommend to the readers of Hypogeum, even though it doesn’t belong to the musical genres I usually write about. It might be a different kind of music, or not even music at all; maybe fine arts or literature. This column will be the place for all these random recommendations and reviews. Let’s start with an album that doesn’t strictly belong to dark ambient, but is definitely worth your attention. Most of you probably know Peter Bjärgö as the mastermind behind the legendary neoclassical/darkwave project Arcana, or the heavier industrial leanings of Sophia. On his solo work, however, he trades the grand, ritualistic atmosphere for something far more intimate and fragile.

The Translucency of Mind’s Decay is a neoclassical, downtempo release. While it lacks the oppressive drones usually featured on Hypogeum, it shares the exact same nocturnal, melancholic DNA. If you need a break from harsher soundscapes without losing that familiar sense of bleakness, this is the place to start.

# Hollow Press

I have a feeling that I’m preaching to the choir here, but I just can’t skip this. If there’s even one reader among you who hasn’t heard about [Hollow Press](https://hollow-press.net) and realizes what they’ve been missing, my mission is complete. They sell comics, games, and – perhaps their most recognized products – so-called lore game books. This category is a rather peculiar one: they could be described as manuals for games that were never released. The most famous of them, [Vermis](https://hollow-press.net/collections/lore-game-books/products/vermis-i), will be reviewed later in this column if you still need convincing. In the meantime, I suggest you just go to their website and order something. Anything. You won’t be disappointed.
