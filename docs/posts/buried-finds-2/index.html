Buried Finds 2

Let’s continue the excavation from where we left off in the first post.

Sometimes I just want to lie down in my bed and listen to something that’s a bit “less than music” in the conventional sense, but more than just noise. One of the albums I play on those occasions is Annuminas by Arctica. It’s soothing, like standing on a hill on a cool autumn day and looking at the forest. Almost one hour of warm, smooth drones that can reset my mind. There are three versions of it: the original (actually remastered) one; the extended edition, which contains three more tracks for a running total of about 4 hours; and the enchanted edition, with a softer and lighter sound. All are available on the Bandcamp page.

Hymnambulae is a ritualistic music project by Swedish siblings Pär and Åsa Boström. The album titled Orgelhuset was released 10 years ago, so it truly belongs this column. It can be listened to back-to-back with the previous record, as it shares similar qualities. Slow and engulfing, featuring tape recordings of piano and voices, combined with deep drones.

Let’s stay with one of the siblings, Pär Boström. His project, Aindulmedir, is a bit more in dungeon/winter synth territory, but The Lunar Lexicon is definitely one of my favourite albums. More melodic than my other recommendations, it’s something I regularly return to after listening to deeper, darker ambient for hours. It’s the more soothing and calming kind of dark atmospheric music.

A much more recent release (from October 2025) is Kingdom of Radiant Darkness by Altum. It contains one, 40-minute-long track. Sometimes melodic, sometimes disturbing, but always beautiful and engaging.

The last recommendations is from 2018 and was released by Ruptured World. The album is Frontiers of Disorder, the debut album of Scottish writer and musician Alistair Rennie. This was before the “Exoplanetary” trilogy, which might be (or even should be) familiar to you. It’s a different sound, a bit more cinematic, sometimes closer to space ambient, but definitely worth checking out.
