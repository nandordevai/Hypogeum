Primordial Sounds

I started a couple of new columns in this volume. This one was my oldest idea: let’s go through the most important albums of the dark underground genres. In Primordial Sounds I will recommend a couple of records, along with some information on why are these important.

Lustmord – Heresy (1990)

I don’t subscribe to the notion that there was a definitive “first” album in dark ambient, but we should start somewhere – and what would be better than this record. Before Heresy, the dark underground was often a mix of industrial noise and power electronics. Brian Williams (Lustmord) took those elements and sank them into a vast, subterranean space. Recorded in various locations (crypts, mines, catacombs) Heresy sounds massive and atmospheric. It moved away from the aggression of the 80s industrial scene toward a sense of cosmic dread and physical weight.

Why It Is Essential: It stripped away melody and rhythm in favor of low-frequency oscillations and environmental recordings. Every project working in ritual or cinematic ambient today owes a debt to the acoustic architecture found here.

Key Track: Heresy Part IV – A perfect example of how silence and sub-bass can create more tension than any wall of noise.

Raison d’être – Prospectus I (1993)

While Lustmord explored cosmic and subterranean dread, Peter Andersson’s Raison d’être introduced a "sacral" element to the genre. Prospectus I is the blueprint for Ritual Ambient. The Atmosphere is a heavy blend of Gregorian chants, clanging industrial bells, and sweeping, mournful synths. It feels like standing in a crumbling cathedral in the middle of a nuclear winter.

Why It Is Essential: It proved that dark ambient could be melodic and emotive without losing its grim edge. This album put the Cold Meat Industry label on the map and influenced every ritual project that followed.

Key Track: “Katharos” – The perfect fusion of medieval choral echoes and cold, machine-like hums.

Thomas Köner – Permafrost (1993)

If other artists are “dark”, Köner is “white out”. Permafrost is the ultimate Isolationist record. It is so minimalist that it almost ceases to be music and becomes a physical environment. It is nspired by the desolate, frozen landscapes of the Arctic. The sound is composed of deep, undulating sub-bass and the cracking sounds of ice. It is claustrophobic and vast at the same time.

Why It Is Essential: Köner stripped the genre of all horror tropes and replaced them with the terrifying indifference of nature. It’s a masterclass in sound texture.

Key Track: “Nieve” – A track that makes the listener feel the temperature in the room drop.

Zoviet France – Shadow, Thief of the Sun (1991)

Before the genre was fully codified, Zoviet France was experimenting with lo-fi and tribal elements. This album is a bridge between the experimental industrial 80s and the ambient 90s; it’s rainy, hypnotic, and strange. It uses acoustic instruments, ethnic percussion, and heavy tape manipulation to create a sound that feels ancient rather than futuristic.

Why It Is Essential: It introduced the idea of Tribal Ambient. It’s less about “space” and more about the earth, ruins, and forgotten civilizations. It’s an essential listen for those who prefer an organic, analog grit.

Key Track: “The Third Half” – A swirling, rhythmic descent into a hazy, dusty dreamscape.
