Reviews

I have two long-overdue reviews in the queue – apologies to the artists. Let’s dive into the records and see what makes them great and interesting.

Ordo Serpentis – In Praise Of Darkness

Shane Beck & “the entity known as The Black Monolith” (their own words) released this album on February 13, 2026. It’s their 3rd release, and they recommend it for fans of Atrium Carceri, In Slaughter Natives, Nordvargr, Mortaur, Vestigial, MZ.412, Trepaneringsritualen, Swans, and Ulver. It definitely sounded interesting, so I had high hopes for this release (especially knowing some of their earlier works).

It starts with the title track: drones and spoken word slowly building the atmosphere for almost 12 minutes into a noisy, chaotic piece.

We continue with ‘Society Of The Obscene’, featuring unnerving synthesizer sounds paired again with spoken samples. I’m starting to get the feeling of being trapped in some kind of strange movie, where the noises and the soundtrack have completely taken over the script, with lonely actors delivering monologues and stumbling around the oversized scenery.

The scene changes in ‘When Night Enveloped Me’: it starts with abrasive drones, summoning the image of swarms of post-apocalyptic robot locusts. For the second part of the track it softens a bit, and another monologue starts.

In ‘The Whispering Of The Blade’ we continue with some lighter ambience, though it’s no less intense. Paired with the noise of the second part, it really plays into the techno-spiritual feeling of the album. The light that we see at the end of the tunnel is definitely the glow of some infernal machine.

The noise returns in ‘Serpent Of The Blood Cult’ along with some heavily distorted, rhythmic industrial samples. The hypnotic pattern continues throughout the whole track, until you start feeling like you belong to the Blood Cult. I can’t wait to find out where this crazy journey leads me with the closing track.

‘Perversions Of The Flame’ doesn’t start with any relief. It introduces some melodic pads, but the dissonance always creeps back in, along with the noises and the monologues.

As the song progresses, the semi-rhythmic noises start to dominate, before we finally end on some strange pads – not getting the release we were craving, but maybe the one we needed. As the artists say themselves: “And these murky pathways that seem to descend eternally. Allowing the music to embrace you & be entwined in it's darkest depths. Understanding there is nothing but freedom in the voice that calls you.”

La Compagnie du Crépuscule

Our next one is the self-titled album by a French ethno-dark-ambient duo. It features 22 tracks ranging from 39 seconds to over 6 minutes, so I won’t write a track-by-track review. I’ll let Étienne, one of the members, introduce the project first:

“We are influenced by the french ethno-ambient scene of the 90's that you maybe know, bands like Désaccord Majeur, Internal Fusion, Tlön Uqbar and so on. As well as older bands like Popol Vuh. Despite these influences, the entire album is made or free improvisations with microKORG, Yamaha CS Reface, a sampler for ambient sounds and a few softwares on a pad. Recorded straight to a digital recorder. Hence the unpolished but naive and sincere feel of the album.”

It’s definitely not easy listening. You have to put in some effort to discover the details, but I hope for the readers of Hypogeum that’s not a negative. The album is a mesmerizing amalgamation of all the mentioned influences and much more. There are tracks that are closer to dungeon synth (Nuit I), others are more ritualistic (Escale), and there’s a dark ambient streak as well (À l'aveuglette). All in all, this is a truly unique and convincing release. It’s available for free on Bandcamp.
